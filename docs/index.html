Money in Congress

STOCK Act filings are public — members of Congress must disclose trades within 45 days. We backtested 10,000+ trades from 170 members against SPY. A 90-day follow-disclosure strategy outperforms by +2.4% on average — and the edge grows with time.

Limitations & caveats

Backtesting assumptions

All returns assume buying at the closing price on the filing date with no slippage or liquidity constraints. In practice, some of these tickers are illiquid and execution costs will reduce returns. The backtest also covers 2022–2026 — a strong bull market. Results in different rate or volatility regimes may differ.

Data accuracy

Congressional disclosures are self-reported with known errors and omissions. The live feed (Quiver Quant) notes their data "may be inaccurate or incomplete." Options strike and expiry data is parsed from free-text description fields and can misparse. The 30–45 day disclosure lag means the actual trade is already history by the time you see it.

Not financial advice

This is a research and education project. Congressional disclosure patterns are one signal derived from public STOCK Act filings — not a trading strategy guarantee. The 12 "reliable" politicians were selected in-sample from the same 2022–2026 data the backtest runs on (tightened from an initial screen of 37 to require avg excess >2% and ≥20 trades). Even with stricter criteria, in-sample selection inflates apparent returns. Sample sizes per politician are small; high excess returns may reflect a specific period or sector rather than persistent edge. Do your own analysis before making any investment decisions.

What's next

Validation TODOs

The backtest signal is intriguing but not yet validated. These four tests are needed before the edge can be considered real rather than an artefact of in-sample selection.

Walk-forward test

Not run

Rank politicians on 2022–2023 data only, then run the backtest on 2024–2026 without touching the training split. If the same names outperform out-of-sample, that's genuine signal — not a noise survivor from selecting in-sample.

Permutation test

Not run

Randomly shuffle politician→ticker assignments and rerun the backtest 1,000 times to build a null distribution. If the observed +2.3% excess sits in the top 5% of that distribution, it's unlikely to be noise.

Transaction cost sensitivity

Not run

Rerun at 0.25%, 0.5%, and 1.0% round-trip friction. The backtest assumes zero slippage and no bid-ask spread. At +2.3% average excess, even modest execution costs could materially erode the edge.

Risk-adjusted returns

Not run

Compute the beta of the follow-congress portfolio vs SPY and derive alpha. If excess returns vanish after beta adjustment, the strategy is holding higher-volatility stocks — not exploiting an informational edge.

Under the hood

How it works

1 · Scrape

Download every PTR filing PDF from disclosures-clerk.house.gov and HTML filings from efdsearch.senate.gov. Parse transaction tables with pdfplumber. ~10,000 trades, 2022–2026.

2 · Backtest

Entry at close on ReportDate (the filing date, not the trade date). Fetch OHLCV prices with yfinance. Measure excess return vs SPY over 10d / 20d / 30d / 60d / 90d hold windows.

3 · Signal

Live disclosures via Quiver Quant API (12h cache). Filter to the 12 reliable politicians (avg excess >2%, ≥20 trades). Deduplicate accumulation signals. Detect deep-ITM call purchases as underlying buy signals.

4 · Execute

Place orders via Alpaca paper trading API. 5% of equity per position, max 15 open. State persisted in JSON across runs. Exit triggered by planned_exit date (filing date + hold days).
